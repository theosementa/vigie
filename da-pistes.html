Radar · art-direction tracks

Three ways to keep a watch.

Each track starts from a different metaphor and has its own name, palette, typography and reference theme. Switch Dark / Light at the top: every track exists in both. You can pick one or mix them (“A's name, B's typeface”).

The objects

A radar screen and its echoes, a press clipping, a highlighter, a stack of magazines, a queue of tapes, a tide gauge.

The gestures

Sharing from Safari or YouTube on the metro, one hand. Emptying the pile in the evening on the couch. Sorting twenty tabs at the desk, with a mouse.

The vocabulary

Watch, signal, noise, to watch, seen, pile, queue, glean, send to. No “feed”, no “like”: nothing that pushes you to scroll.

The references

Marine navigation screens at night, printed press reviews, tide gauges. We take principles from them, no logo or brand element.

Track A · dark reference theme

Vigie

The vigie is the lookout watching from the top of the mast. Metaphor: the screen of a navigation instrument at night. What comes in is an echo, what you have seen fades out.

A watch instrument, not a news feed.

87to watch

Geologica, a single variable family: tight 600 for titles, 500 for numbers, 400 for text. Its slightly technical shapes evoke a dial without falling into monospace.

The echo (phosphor green) marks only three things: the Add action, what is new (unopened share, today's addition), the “now” point of charts.
What it says
  • Serious, precise, nocturnal: you monitor your watch like an instrument.
  • Goal rings become the radar screen, with no gimmick.
Its risk
  • Phosphor green on black borders on the “hacker” cliché if it spills over.
  • Less warm for those who do not come from tech.

Name: Vigie (other option: Balise).

Short, pronounceable in English as in French, says exactly what the app does: keep watch. Rarely used as a software name.

Track B · light reference theme

Glane

To glean is to gather what is left after the harvest, one ear at a time. Metaphor: your personal press review. You cut out, highlight what you read, file.

A press review of your own, not a feed.

Newsreader (optical-size serif) for article titles and numbers: we read titles, they should look like titles. Public Sans for the rest of the interface.

The highlighter marks three things: what you are reading now, the Add action, the current period in charts. Never as a card background, never as text.
What it says
  • Reading, calm, culture: watching as a magazine you leaf through.
  • Titles are the heroes; a card without an image still looks good.
Its risk
  • Yellow must stay rare, otherwise you get a board of sticky notes.
  • Videos read less well in a “paper” register.

Name: Glane (other option: Coupure).

Close to English glean, understood on both sides. A gentle verb that says we choose, one thing at a time.

Funnel Display at a very tight 700 for the pile level and screen titles, Funnel Sans for the interface. The pile number is the biggest in the app, everywhere.

The sea is a fill, not an accent: one per screen, always for the pile level. The buoy (orange) marks the Add action and the “now” point.
What it says
  • Energetic, motivating: the app shows you that you are moving forward, not what you are missing.
  • One strong image per screen, immediately recognisable.
Its risk
  • The whole app rests on the pile: if it rises, the home screen makes you feel guilty.
  • The fill takes discipline so it does not become a banner.

Name: Ebb (other option: Jusant, the French word).

Three letters, an obvious icon (a waterline and a buoy). In French, to be explained once.
